## I. Pastoral Heritage Subverted : From Shepherds' Shelter to Luxury Villa

Throughout Corsica, from the scrubland of Cap Corse to the granite ridges of the Alta Rocca, dry-stone shepherd huts (*i pagliaghji*) bear testament to centuries of pastoral adaptation to the Mediterranean landscape. Built from local schist or granite, these modest structures historically housed shepherds and flocks during seasonal transhumance. Located frequently within pristine wild areas of breathtaking natural beauty — in the heart of strictly unbuildable remarkable spaces protected by Coastal Law and PADDUC —, these historical ruins have become primary targets for speculative investors.

Under the guise of restoring traditional architectural heritage, private SCIs acquire these dilapidated ruins for low sums, then execute radical conversion operations into hyper-luxurious secondary residences outfitted with ultra-modern comforts.

## II. The Loophole of Article L. 151-11 of the Urban Planning Code

The legal mechanism leveraged to achieve this conversion lies in Article L. 151-11 of the French Urban Planning Code. This article authorizes in agricultural zones (Zone A) or natural zones (Zone N) the **restoration and limited extension of existing buildings** formally identified in local municipal urban plans (PLU) or communal maps.

Savvy developers submit building permit applications describing « identical restoration of a traditional pastoral hut ». In reality, the work involves tearing down the original dry-stone walls to build concrete-framed modern villas clad in stone veneer, augmented with panoramic timber decks, massive water reservoirs, underground swimming pools, and unpermitted private sewage tanks. The estate is subsequently advertised on international luxury vacation rental portals at rates reaching 15,000 € to 30,000 € per week during the summer season.

> « Converting simple dry-stone shepherd huts lacking water or electricity into hyper-equipped luxury villas inside protected natural spaces constitutes a blatant subversion of Article L. 151-11 of the Urban Planning Code. »

## III. The Role of SAFER and the Lack of Effective Preemption

Why does SAFER de Corse (Land Development and Rural Settlement Agency) fail to preempt these huts and reserve them for young shepherds establishing working herds? The reason is financial: market prices commanded by these ruins once boasting panoramic sea views become completely detached from agricultural land valuation tables published by the Ministry of Agriculture.

Lacking dedicated public matching funds, SAFER is arithmetically unable to outbid wealthy speculative buyers.

## VI. Forensic Analysis of Administrative Decrees & Rural Planning Guidelines

Scrutiny of rural conversion permits reveals systemic circumvention:

1. **Analysis of Existing Building Identification Criteria :** Municipalities routinely classify collapsed piles of stone lacking roofs or four intact walls as « existing buildings », violating Council of State case law.
2. **Scrutiny of Secondary Swimming Pool Authorizations :** Town halls approve swimming pools and ancillary annexes under the guise of « firefighting water retention ponds » (*Réserves Incendie*).
3. **Audit of Short-Term Commercial Tourist Rental Registrations :** Converted agricultural huts operate as luxury boutique hotels without declaring commercial hospitality activities or paying tourist tax.

## VII. Citizen FOIA (CADA) Recourse Framework for Pastoral Preservation

Under Article L. 311-1 CRPA, citizens can demand:

1. **Certified Municipal Architectural Inventories (Mairies) :** Graphic annexes defining Article L. 151-11 eligibility.
2. **DDTM Coastal Law Compliance Opinions (DDTM) :** Legality reviews on building permits granted in remarkable spaces.
3. **SPANC Sanitation Inspection Audits (Intercommunalities) :** Wastewater system conformity certificates.
4. **Agricultural Chamber Activity Verification Logs (Chambre d'Agriculture) :** Proof of genuine farming activity on restored plots.
