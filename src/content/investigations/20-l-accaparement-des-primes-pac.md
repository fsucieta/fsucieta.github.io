## I. The TéléPAC System and the Perversion of Per-Hectare Aid

Since the 2015 reform of the Common Agricultural Policy (CAP), farming subsidies shifted from production-based support to decoupled acreage support: Basic Payment Rights (BPR / DPB). The more land an operator declares under the Graphical Parcel Register (RPG), the more European subsidies they pocket, completely detached from actual local food production.

In Corsica, French regulatory authorities granted a unique exemption: **an immediate and total convergence as early as 2015**, setting the unit value of the Corsican DPB at **€144.64 per hectare** (14% higher than the French national average of ~€127). Cumulating the DPB, eco-scheme (~€78), redistributive payment (~€49 on the first 52 ha), and Mountain Natural Constraint Allowance (ICHN, €150 to €200), each hectare of impenetrable Mediterranean scrubland, rock, or cliff generates between **€270 and €420 annually**. Because maintenance costs on these inaccessible parcels are strictly zero, the net margin of subsidy capture reaches 100%.

========================================================================================

  [€150.00]   Mountain ICHN (Less Favoured Area Compensation - ODARC)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## II. Criminal Judgments & OLAF Report: €36 Million Diverted

This speculative structure triggered massive fraudulent schemes confirmed by European oversight bodies and criminal courts:

* **OLAF Final Investigation Report (Ref. OF/2017/0403/A1):** The European Anti-Fraud Office estimated that **€36 million** in CAP direct payments were unlawfully claimed over four years (2015–2018) through an aggressive "acreage hunt" on collective lands, unregistered municipal pastures, and unexploitable mountain cliffs.
* **EU Financial Clearance Litigation (CJEU Judgment C-404/19 P & Decision 2017/2014):** The European Commission initially imposed a 100% flat-rate financial correction on all direct surface aid in Haute-Corse for the 2013–2014 claim years due to systemic collapse of the Integrated Administration and Control System (IACS / SIGC).
* **Bastia Criminal Court Judgment (November 16, 2021):** Conviction of the Vallesi family (former agricultural union leaders and consular officials) for embezzling €750,000 in CAP subsidies between 2015 and 2019 across Lucciana, Piano, and Corscia through fictitious farm splitting.
* **Ajaccio Judicial Court Judgment (October 2022):** Conviction of the Rossi family of Letia for unlawfully pocketing €1,400,000 in CAP aid by splitting a single herd across five artificial corporate entities.
* **European Public Prosecutor’s Office (EPPO) / Paris Judicial Court (June 18, 2026):** Conviction of an incarcerated Corsican organized crime member sentenced to one year in prison, €110,000 restitution to ASP, and a lifetime farming ban for fraudulently claiming €120,025 in CAP payments while behind bars with fictitious bovine herds.
* **EPPO / Paris Criminal Court (September 2026):** Conviction of two company managers and a corporation for €263,000 in fraudulent CAP subsidies for a phantom cattle farm in Corsica, involving forged invoices submitted to ODARC.

## III. The Mechanics of Fictitious Acreage & Ear Tag Trafficking

The forensic exploitation of public records demonstrates three key evasion mechanisms:

1. **Annexation of Titleless Land and Sectional Commons:** Taking advantage of the fact that over 40% of the Corsican cadastral register lacks registered title deeds (legacy of the Miot decrees), operators register public communal pastures under TéléPAC without formal municipal council approval or legal leases.
2. **Corporate Farm Splitting ("Russian Dolls"):** Large family holdings are subdivided into 3 to 5 distinct legal corporations (EARL, SCEA, GAEC) to multiply the redistributive bonus on the first 52 hectares ($52 \times €49 = €2,548$ per entity) and evade the €100,000 European direct payment cap.
3. **Ear Tag Trafficking and "Ghost Herds":** Maintaining deceased animals on the EDE database (BDNI), recycling yellow plastic ear tags, or rotating a single herd of 40 cattle across three municipalities to satisfy on-the-spot physical inspections scheduled 48 hours in advance.

## V. Structural Failures of DDTM & ASP Inspections

Forensic examination of state oversight reveals critical vulnerabilities:
* **Inadequate Physical Inspections:** Only 3.8% to 6.2% of farms are inspected on-site annually. An individual farm is inspected physically only once every 18.7 years on average—triple the 6-year criminal statute of limitations for subsidy fraud.
* **48-Hour Advance Notice Loophole:** Enables fraudulent operators to borrow ear-tagged cattle from complicit neighbors and erect temporary pens before state inspectors arrive.
* **Isolated Databases:** Zero automated real-time cross-checking between the ASP payment database (TéléPAC), the national livestock registry (BDNI / EDE), and state slaughterhouse records (FranceAgriMer). A farm can declare 200 cows, collect subsidies for five consecutive years, and show zero slaughtered calves and zero commercial revenue.
* **Inspector Intimidation:** Physical threats, gunshots fired at official inspection vehicles, and administrative pullback from remote mountainous areas without armed police escort.
