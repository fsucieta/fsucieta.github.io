## 💡 CHAPEAU

Inaugurated with great fanfare in October 2025 to link Saint-Joseph to Mezzavia, the 52-million-euro « Angelo » urban cable car conceals one of the most revealing schemes of modern state capitalism. Behind the ecological facade of an aerial transit line, forensic analysis of the 86 pages of the prefectoral DUP order and the budgets of the Communauté d'Agglomération du Pays Ajaccien (CAPA) uncovers a two-pronged strategy: erecting a political monument while organizing the financial asphyxiation of the historic public operator (SPL Muvitarra) to deliver the Ajaccio transit market to a national private corporate giant.

---

## ACT I. THE STATE ALLIANCE & TECHNICAL PROFILE DEVOID OF PASSENGERS

On paper, the « Angelo » urban cable car is presented as a decarbonized mobility masterpiece. Examination of the official dossier validated by prefectoral DUP order No. 2A-2023-12-04-00001 establishes its exact physical parameters: an electrically driven **2.9 km monocable detachable aerial line**, the erection of **19 structural anchor pylons across natural hillsides** (standing between 5 and 30 meters high, including 6 erected in immediate proximity to stations), and the development of **4 stations in total** (Saint-Joseph, Stiletto, Mezzavia, and a 4th intermediate station at « Château d'eau » on former military land to serve the upper sector).

Yet, independent ridership forecasts quickly exposed the operational absurdity of the alignment. The Angelo project was never conceived to meet genuine commuter needs: it served above all as **the political centerpiece of a state alliance between Édouard Philippe and Laurent Marcangeli**. To establish the Horizons party as the pillar of republican stability in Corsica, its local leader required a showcase of modern engineering.

To finance it, the French central state granted an unprecedented subsidy rate of **70% through the Plan de Transformation et d'Investissement pour la Corse (PTIC)**, supplemented by over €6 million in European ERDF funds. Drafted directly inside Matignon transport working groups by shadow technical advisors (Damien Cazé, Jimmy Brun), this financing locked up €26.7 million in public state funds for an infrastructure universally questioned by transit experts.

---

## ACT II. THE CAISSE DES DÉPÔTS / POMA PRIVATE CONCESSION ANNUITY

How did this initially estimated €35 million project surge past **€52.4 million including tax**, paired with a **€23.89 million 10-year operating concession**?

The 86-page public inquiry report listed on the prefectoral register confirms that the review was conducted over 32 consecutive days (from May 30 to June 30, 2023) by **Mr. Gérard PERFETTINI**, Chief Inquiry Commissioner appointed by the President of the Administrative Court of Bastia. While his final opinion approved the DUP, the Commissioner attached two strict administrative reservations and three recommendations regarding safety and surface transit integration.

The Design-Build-Operate-Maintain (CREM) contract brought together Grenoble-based industrialist **POMA** and engineering group **Egis Rail**. Yet shareholder registries reveal a major institutional conflict of interest:
1. **The Caisse des Dépôts (via Banque des Territoires)** advises, finances, and subsidizes the Ajaccio regional authority (CAPA).
2. **The same Caisse des Dépôts is the 75% majority owner of Egis Rail**, the private co-contractor drafting project specifications and collecting engineering fees alongside POMA!

The state financial institution thus sat on both sides of the table: approving public loans with one hand while pocketing private engineering margins with the other. The signed contract guarantees **SECA** (a 100% POMA subsidiary) a fixed annual annuity of **€2.38 million per year over 10 years (€23.89M before tax)**. With actual ridership stalling below **18%** (commuters rejecting cumbersome bus transfers), the annual operating deficit exceeds **€1.98 million per year**, representing an indirect cost of **815.50 euros per Ajaccio taxpaying household**.

---

## ACT III. BEHIND THE SCENES OF THE ROUTE : COST OVERRUNS, LAND & THE ADMINISTRATIVE LOCK

On the ground, construction triggered a cascade of hidden cost overruns and land maneuvers:
- **Soil contamination at the Saint-Joseph military site** : Earthworks at the lower terminal uncovered hydrocarbon pollution on former navy fuel grounds, requiring deep-pile foundations (**+€2.4 million in amendments**).
- **Helicopter lifting on Mont Sant’Anghjulu** : To anchor pylons P5 to P12 on steep roadless hillsides, heavy twin-turbine Super Puma helicopters were mobilized, causing a logistical surcharge exceeding **€450,000**.
- **Emergency acquisition of the Viguier Sawmill** : At Stiletto, securing the intermediate station required the rushed buyout of private sawmill land to avoid judicial obstruction, creating an **emergency land debt of €3 million**.
- **The overhead airspace easement trap (SUP)** : By decree of December 4, 2023, the Prefecture imposed Public Overflight Easements authorizing cable cars over private villas, restricting property rights without fair compensation.
- **Partitioning citizen opposition registries** : The opening order of May 10, 2023 reveals the Prefecture set up two segregated digital portals (**No. 4664 for DUP/PLU** and **No. 4665 for land expropriations**), deliberately separating overflown homeowner complaints from commuter feedback.
- **Real estate entanglement at Stiletto** : Article 5 of the DUP imposed avoidance measures for the **Hermann's Tortoise**, while revealing (page 3) that the alignment connects directly with private real estate interests: the order of Jan 27, 2021 granted protected species dispensations to « Les Terrasses du Stiletto », developed by **a highly influential regional construction conglomerate**.

## ACT IV. THE COLLAPSE OF LEGALITY CONTROL AND THE TROJAN HORSE ON MUVITARRA

How could such a mechanism be endorsed by state oversight bodies?
During his tenure (2020-2022), Corsican Prefect **Pascal Lelarge** demonstrated extreme severity in local administrative oversight. Yet under his signature, the 70% PTIC state financing conventions were rubber-stamped without any formal warnings regarding financial risk. Meanwhile, the **Regional Court of Accounts (CRC)** remained silent, issuing no alarms regarding the cost surge (from €35M to €52.4M) or the operational transfer of risk to SECA.

It was on this weakened terrain that the financial strangulation of **SPL Muvitarra** was orchestrated. Historically balanced, the public transit company was starved by CAPA. To absorb the €2.38M annual fee owed to POMA/SECA and fund prefectoral obligations, local authorities withheld earned revenues and slashed neighborhood bus operating budgets.

---

## ACT V. THE SOCIAL CRISIS OF DECEMBER 2025 AND THE PRIVATIZATION AMBUSH

The material reality of this budget crisis erupted into daylight on **December 2, 2025** during the general strike paralyzing the Muvistrada network. By September 2024, CAPA had unilaterally terminated employee collective agreements, issuing an ultimatum to slash bus driver working conditions labeled as « financially unsustainable ».

Yet while CAPA cited austerity to impose wage cuts on public workers, it continued paying the guaranteed **€2.38M annual rent to private concessionaire POMA/SECA** for cable cars running nearly empty. Following weeks of depot blockades led by STC, CGT, and FO unions, CAPA decreed emergency fare-free transit until January 5, 2026.

*Investigative legal analysis*: While a public authority may compensate commuters for service disruption, granting massive fare-free transit funded by taxpayers months before municipal elections borders on violating **Article L. 52-1 of the French Electoral Code**, prohibiting the use of public resources for electoral promotion.

This appeasement maneuver concealed the real roadmap: dissolving the public operator to transition to a **private Public Service Delegation (DSP)**. If this market is captured by a **national private corporate giant**, Ajaccio taxpayers' money will be siphoned to mainland corporate desks, repeating the historical disaster experienced with the **Transdev** group before workers mobilized to restore public control.
