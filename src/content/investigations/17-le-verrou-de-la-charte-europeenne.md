## I. The Dogma of Article 2 of the Constitution : The Monolingual Republic

Since the 1992 constitutional amendment inserting the phrase « The language of the Republic is French » into Article 2 of the French Constitution, the central state and Constitutional Council have maintained an absolute legal barrier against any official recognition of the Corsican language. Every resolution passed by the Assembly of Corsica establishing **co-officiality** (equal legal standing of Corsican and French in public administration, courts, and civic life) is systematically challenged and struck down by state prefects.

Even the landmark Molac Law on the heritage protection of regional languages was partially censored by the Constitutional Council in May 2021, unconstitutionally restricting immersive bilingual teaching in public schools under the pretext that it infringed upon the mandatory supremacy of French.

## II. France's Non-Ratification of the European Charter

France remains one of the few member states of the Council of Europe refusing to ratify the **European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages**, signed in 1999 but blocked by the Council of State and Constitutional Council. This charter guarantees fundamental cultural rights: regional language schooling, its usage before local authorities and courts, and guaranteed presence in public broadcasting.

This refusal isolates Corsica from modern European cultural diversity standards enforced in Catalonia, the Basque Country, Wales, South Tyrol, and the Åland Islands.

> « A language that is not taught universally and is banned from official civic life is a language condemned to become mere folkloric decoration for tourists. »

## III. The Reality of Linguistic Decline and the Urgency of General Bilingualism

Sociolinguistic surveys conducted by INSEE and the University of Corsica expose an emergency: the proportion of fluent native speakers is declining with each generation. While seniors born before 1960 predominantly master the language, family transmission was broken by decades of institutional school francization.

Without mandatory immersive schooling from preschool through secondary education, the Corsican language faces irreversible extinction as a living community tongue within two generations.

## VI. Forensic Analysis of Administrative Decrees & Linguistic Censorship

Scrutiny of state administrative decrees reveals persistent hostility toward regional language parity:

1. **Analysis of Prefectoral Annulment Petitions Against Mayors :** Prefects routinely file administrative appeals against town councils conducting debates in Corsican, citing Article 2 of the Constitution.
2. **Scrutiny of National Education Immersion Restrictions :** Ministerial circulars restrict immersive pedagogy to private associative networks, denying public school children equal linguistic immersion.
3. **Audit of Administrative Court Jurisprudence (Bastia) :** Courts enforce absolute French monolingualism, invalidating municipal public signage containing only Corsican toponyms.
