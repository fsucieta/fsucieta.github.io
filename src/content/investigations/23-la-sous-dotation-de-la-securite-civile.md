## I. Wildfire Vulnerability and Defective Aerial Pre-Positioning

Every summer, Corsica's ancient Laricio pine forests, dense scrubland, and steep mountain valleys are exposed to extreme or exceptional wildfire danger driven by high heat and gale-force Libecciu winds. Rapid aerial water bombing within the initial 10 minutes of ignition represents the sole decisive factor preventing catastrophic mega-fires.

Yet, the central aerial deployment strategy managed from the national base in Nîmes-Garons systematically leaves Corsica vulnerable. Instead of permanently stationing a dedicated squadron of Canadair CL-415 and Dash 8 water bombers at Solenzara Air Base and Ajaccio Airport throughout the entire summer, Paris makes day-to-day tactical reallocations, frequently leaving the island with only 1 or 2 aircraft to cover 8,700 km² of rugged terrain.

## II. Financial Precariousness of SIS 2A and SIS 2B Firefighters

On the ground, the two Fire and Rescue Services (SIS 2A and SIS 2B) depend over 80% on the dedication of volunteer firefighters (*sapeurs-pompiers volontaires*). These brave men and women, often rural workers and farmers, staff aging rescue stations across interior valleys.

The operational budgets of the SIS are funded through mandatory contributions levied on local municipalities and the Collectivité de Corse. Meanwhile, the summer influx of 3 million tourists triples emergency interventions (car crashes, sea rescues, hiking traumas on the GR20) without any state budgetary equalization to cover the surge in vehicle wear, equipment, and fuel costs.

> « High-mountain rescues on the GR20 and summer wildfire combat are conducted with civil protection budgets sized for a small winter population. Corsican firefighters compensate for state under-funding with raw bravery. »

## III. The Dragon 20 Helicopters and PGHM Mountain Rescue Overstretch

In the high massifs (Monte Cinto, Monte Rotondo, Aiguilles de Bavella), specialized search and rescue operations are carried out jointly by the PGHM (High Mountain Gendarmerie Platoon) and fire department mountain rescue teams (GMS), transported by Civil Protection Dragon 20 helicopters and Gendarmerie Choucas aircraft.

However, mechanical breakdowns, aging aircraft frames, and lack of replacement helicopters during scheduled maintenance periods regularly leave the island without immediate heavy airborne medical evacuation capacity.

## VI. Forensic Analysis of Administrative Decrees & Civil Safety Directives

Scrutiny of civil defense decrees reveals systemic deficiencies:

1. **Analysis of National Civil Aircraft Maintenance Contracts :** Outsourced private fleet maintenance contracts have led to recurring groundings of Canadair aircraft during peak summer months.
2. **Scrutiny of Municipal Fire Contribution Formulas :** Rural communes with declining populations are legally forced to pay rising SIS quotas to subsidize coastal tourist emergency coverage.
3. **Audit of Mountain Rescue Protocols :** Legal ambiguities between military gendarmerie and civil firefighter dispatch create response latency during high-altitude distress calls.

## VII. Citizen FOIA (CADA) Recourse Framework for Emergency Safety

Under Article L. 311-1 CRPA, citizens can demand:

1. **Certified Ministry Aircraft Readiness Sheets (DGSCGC) :** Daily aircraft availability figures during July and August.
2. **Fire and Rescue Equipment Depreciation Ledgers (SIS 2A/2B) :** Average vehicle age and replacement schedules.
3. **Mountain Rescue Emergency Flight Logs (Prefecture) :** Average dispatch delay times for high-altitude medical evacuations.
4. **Natural Hazard Prevention Plan Execution Audits (DDTM) :** Municipal wildfire and flood risk management compliance.

## VIII. Institutional Actors & Networks of Influence Mapping

The investigation into **Civil Protection** reveals key institutional entities:

- **DGSCGC / Ministry of the Interior :** Controlling the national aerial fleet with centralized mainland dispatch priorities.
- **SIS 2A & SIS 2B (Fire and Rescue Services) :** Local emergency operators bearing the physical burden of interventions.
- **PGHM & Gendarmerie Mountain Platoons :** Highly skilled high-altitude search and rescue operators.
- **Collectivité de Corse (CdC) :** Primary funder of rescue infrastructure without holding statutory command over state aerial assets.
- **Volunteer Firefighters & Mountain Guides :** Civil society pillars defending territorial safety with limited means.
