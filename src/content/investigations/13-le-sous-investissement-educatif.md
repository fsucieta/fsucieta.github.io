## I. The Heritage of Pasquale Paoli and the Battle for Insular Knowledge

In 1765, at the zenith of the Corsican Republic, General of the Nation Pasquale Paoli founded the University of Corte, enshrining the pioneering principle that political freedom and sovereignty depend upon education, scientific inquiry, and the cultivation of local elites. Brute conquest shuttered the institution after the Battle of Ponte-Novu in 1769, and it took a historic popular mobilization in 1981 to compel Paris to reopen its doors.

Today, the Università Pasquale Paoli educates over 4,700 students across Corte, Bastia, and Ajaccio. It stands as the primary engine of social mobility and scientific innovation on insular themes (renewable energy, ecological engineering, Corsican language and culture, comparative territorial law).

## II. Ministerial Under-Funding and Research Post Freezes

However, analysis of Ministry of Higher Education (MESR) allocation algorithms (the SYMPA / SYMPA 2 model) exposes deep structural bias favoring large metropolitan universities.

Due to its human-scale size and insular geography (which creates structural surcharges for laboratory equipment imports and academic travel), the University of Corsica receives an annual per-student allocation nearly 25% below the French mainland average. Advanced academic tracks in engineering, health sciences, and digital technology lack tenured professorships, forcing over-reliance on underpaid adjunct lecturers.

> « Over 55% of Corsican high school graduates pursue higher education on the mainland. Lacking specialized master's programs and senior career opportunities on the island, the majority of these young minds never return. »

## III. The Brain Drain and the Student Housing Crisis in Corte

To academic under-funding is added severe student housing pressure. In Corte, the student population accounts for over half the town's winter inhabitants. Rents have surged due to speculative pressure, while public CROUS student dormitories meet less than 28% of verified demand.

Furthermore, the lack of private research & development (R&D) investments on the island drives a continuous brain drain, depriving Corsica of the engineering talent required for its ecological and economic transition.

## VI. Forensic Analysis of Administrative Decrees & Higher Education Policy

Scrutiny of higher education decrees reveals ongoing centralizing constraints:

1. **Analysis of National Degree Accreditation Decrees :** Central ministry committees enforce standardized criteria that obstruct tailored bilingual and insular curriculum development.
2. **Scrutiny of CROUS Capital Investment Budgets :** State funding for student accommodation in Corte has lagged inflation by over 35% since 2015.
3. **Audit of Regional Research Autonomy :** Research grants from European Horizon programs are routed through centralized Parisian agencies, creating administrative overhead.
