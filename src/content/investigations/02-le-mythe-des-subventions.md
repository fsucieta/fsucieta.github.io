## 1. AUTOPSY OF THE TAX INVOICE : THE €420 MILLION PRIMARY SURPLUS

Examine an income tax notice, take a grocery receipt in Ajaccio, or look at the receipt from filling up your fuel tank in Bastia or Porto-Vecchio. Everywhere, you pay VAT (at 2.1%, 10%, or 20%), personal income tax (IRPP), and domestic fuel excise taxes (TICPE).

The question rarely asked in Paris is elementary: **where does this money go, and how much genuinely returns to the island?**

Budget execution records from the Directorate General of Public Finances (DGFiP) and French Customs provide the exact balance of payments:

* **What Corsica transmits to the central state:** **€1.45 billion per year**.
  * VAT collected from local households and 3 million seasonal tourists: ~€750 million.
  * Personal income tax from residents: ~€290 million.
  * Domestic fuel and energy excise taxes (TICPE): ~€240 million.
  * Registration fees, inheritance duties, and real estate taxes: ~€170 million.
* **What the central state actually spends on civilian services in Corsica:** **€1.03 billion per year**.
  * Salaries of civilian state personnel (teachers, court clerks, prefecture officers, tax inspectors).
  * General operating grants (DGF) distributed to municipalities and the Territorial Collectivity of Corsica.
  * Direct state civilian operational interventions on the island.

$$\text{Primary Operating Fiscal Balance} = €1,450\text{M} - €1,030\text{M} = \mathbf{+€420 \text{ Million / year}}$$

> ⚖️ **The Examining Magistrate's Finding :**  
> Every year, the central government extracts a **net profit of €420 million from Corsica**, allocated directly toward balancing national budget deficits in Paris. This corresponds to **€1,181 per year for every resident**, and **€2,553 net per year for every taxpaying household in Corsica**.  
> This initial baseline metric completely invalidates the stereotype of 'subsidized Corsica'. Yet it represents merely the visible tip of the extraction mechanism.

## 2. BERCY'S LEVY ON LOCAL COMMUNES : ARTICLE 1641 OF THE CGI

Very few citizens are aware that the French Ministry of Economy & Finance applies a direct surcharge on local municipal taxes voted by Corsican mayors to maintain elementary schools or drinking water pipelines.

Under **Article 1641 of the French General Tax Code (CGI)**, Bercy levies a 'service fee' for collecting local municipal taxes:
* **3%** on residential property taxes (1% administrative fee + 2% relief provision).
* **8%** (4.4% + 3.6%) on the Household Waste Collection Tax (TEOM)!
* Across all municipal property taxes paid in Corsica (~€620M), **Bercy captures €26.04 million per year in pure collection commissions**.

To this must be added the state's revenue from insular real estate speculation:
* In Corsica, **38% of all housing units are secondary holiday residences (95,000 secondary homes, the highest ratio in Europe)** (Insee Focus No. 247).
* On each speculative real estate transaction, the central government deducts:
  * State Real Estate Registration Tax (0.715%): **€14.30M / year**.
  * Real Estate Security Contribution (CSI, Art. 879 CGI at 0.10%): **€2.00M / year**.
  * Handling commission on municipal transfer duties: **€2.13M / year**.
  * Capital gains taxes and social surcharges (CSG/CRDS) on secondary residence sales: **~€35.00M / year**.

$$\text{Total Local Tax Dues & Land Speculation Fees Seized by Bercy} = \mathbf{€79.47 \text{ Million / year}}$$

> 💡 **In plain words for your household :**  
> You pay elevated waste collection taxes to dispose of refuse left by millions of tourists during summer? **The central government takes an 8% commission directly on your household trash bill**! And on every luxury villa resold with substantial capital gains to non-residents, Paris collects the tax while Corsican youths are priced out of land ownership.

## 3. THE MYTH OF THE 'EXCEPTIONAL INVESTMENT PROGRAM' (PEI) : 600% SELF-FINANCED

Whenever Corsican representatives demand fiscal parity, the standard continental political reply is invoked: *'Corsica received the €2 billion Exceptional Investment Program (PEI), you should be grateful!'*

Examining the PEI through the official audit of the Court of Accounts reveals the deception:
1. **Spread over 20 years :** The €2 billion was never paid as a lump sum. It was diluted from 2002 to 2022, representing an average of **€100M per year** across all co-funders.
2. **State funding capped at 70% :** The central government actually paid an annual average of only **€70 million per year**.
3. **The Comparative Arithmetic Reality :**
   * What the state paid annually under the PEI: **€70M / year**.
   * What Bercy extracted in net surplus from Corsica: **€420M / year**.

$$\text{Self-Financing Ratio} = \frac{€420\text{M}}{€70\text{M}} = \mathbf{6.00}$$

> ⚖️ **The Auditor's Verdict :**  
> While the state disbursed €70M annually to renovate aging roads and pipes with decades of delay, **it extracted €420M per year in net fiscal surplus from insular taxpayers**.  
> **The central government took six times more net fiscal surplus than it reinvested under the 'Exceptional Program'!** Corsica received no state handout: insular taxpayers funded 100% of their public infrastructure while generating **€350 million in annual net cash profit for the central Treasury in Paris**.

## 4. THE TRANSPORT AND ENERGY BURDEN (SENATE REPORTS 488 & 714)

### The Territorial Continuity Grant Frozen Since 2009 (DOC-01, pages 59-60)
The Territorial Continuity Grant (DCT) is mandated by **Article L. 4425-26 of the CGCT** to offset maritime insularity and guarantee equal mobility rights.
* In 2009, the budget was capped at **€187 million per year**.
* In 2026, the grant remains **frozen at exactly €187 million**.
* Yet cumulative inflation recorded by Insee between 2009 and 2026 stands at **+41.84%**.
* Under normal statutory indexation, the allocation should reach **€265.24 million per year**.
* **Annual shortfall inflicted on the Collectivity of Corsica:** **€78.24 million per year** (or **€475 per household**).
* **Cumulative financial loss certified (2009-2026):** **€664.60 million**.

### The Heavy Fuel Oil Monopoly and Tax-Free Bunkers
The central government claims to allocate **€450 to €500 million per year** in CSPE energy equalization to Corsica (Senate Report No. 714).
* **Who receives this subsidy?** This funding **is never paid to Corsican families**. It is credited directly to the accounts of **EDF Systèmes Énergétiques Insulaires (EDF SEI)** to guarantee 100% cost recovery for its outdated, polluting heavy fuel oil turbines at Vazzio and Lucciana (47.7% of the island's electricity production).
* **Confiscated hydroelectric rent:** Meanwhile, Corsica's historic hydroelectric dams (Castirla, Sampolo, Tolla, Rizzanese), fully amortized decades ago, produce clean electricity at less than **€25 per MWh**, absorbed into EDF's national balance sheet without lowering resident bills.
* **The fiscal paradox:** While every Corsican pays €0.60 in excise taxes per liter of fuel for daily transport, the state grants a **100% exemption from excise taxes and VAT on maritime bunker fuels and aviation kerosene** (Articles L. 312-48 and L. 312-55 CIBS). On the 150,000 tons of heavy bunker fuel and 80,000 tons of jet fuel consumed serving the island, the central state provides **€140 million per year in tax breaks to ferry and airline conglomerates**, polluting port cities without territorial retribution.

## 5. STATUTORY INEQUALITY WRITTEN INTO LAW : ARTICLE L. 2334-7 OF THE CGCT

Financial discrimination against Corsica is not an administrative glitch: it is inscribed word-for-word in French law.

Consult **Article L. 2334-7 of the General Code of Local Authorities (1° of I)**, establishing the statutory formula for the state's municipal operating grant (DGF):
* Communes under 500 inhabitants: **€64.46 per inhabitant**.
* Large continental metropolises: **€128.93 per inhabitant**.

$$\text{Operating Grant Ratio} = \frac{€128.93}{€64.46} = \mathbf{2.00 \text{ (+100% for continental metropolises)}}$$

## 6. INVISIBLE EXTRACTION CHANNELS OF CENTRAL TECHNOCRACY

Beyond primary taxes and local government levies, L'OCHJU audited the secondary mechanisms draining insular liquidity:

1. **Fire & Emergency Services Deficit (SIS 2A / 2B) : €45.00M / year**  
   Corsican firefighters battle catastrophic summer wildfires across massive forest canopies. The central government allocates only €9M through motor insurance taxes (TSCA) while the operational budget demands €54M. Insular municipalities must cover the €45M deficit from local tax revenue.
2. **Civil Aviation & Port Infrastructure Fees : €42.00M / year**  
   7.2 million maritime and air passengers transit through Corsican ports and airports annually. Passenger solidarity taxes, civil aviation taxes, and airport security levies flow directly to Paris (DGAC) without maintenance reciprocity.
3. **Corporate Tax Flight of National Chains : €39.81M / year**  
   Supermarket conglomerates, banking branches, and telecommunication operators generate over €2.5 billion in annual turnover on the island. Because profits are consolidated at corporate headquarters in Paris, corporate income tax (IS) is entirely credited outside Corsica.
4. **State Levy on Gambling & Lotteries (FDJ / PMU) : €31.63M / year**  
   Corsica records the highest per capita expenditure on state lotteries and sports betting. The central Treasury siphons 55% of the gross gaming revenue to Paris.
5. **Medical Evacuation & Healthcare Outflow : €25.00M / year**  
   Due to systemic underinvestment in Ajaccio and Bastia hospitals, specialized surgeries and cancer treatments require medical transfers (EVASAN) to Marseille and Nice. The associated social security reimbursement (T2A) is captured by continental university hospitals.
6. **Logistics Penalties & Island Shipping Surcharges : €18.00M / year**  
   Corsican consumers pay private parcel and freight surcharges of €15 to €45 per delivery, draining insular purchasing power.
7. **CAP Agricultural Penalties & Pastoral Land Blocks : €15.00M / year**  
   Administrative disputes over Corsican scrubland grazing eligibility result in European agricultural funding withholdings, penalizing local livestock breeders.
8. **Automated Traffic Cameras (CNT) : €7.50M / year**  
   Fines generated by automated speed cameras across Corsican regional roads flow straight to the National Processing Center in Rennes without local road maintenance reinvestment.
9. **Rhône-Mediterranean Water Basin Levy Surplus : €7.50M / year**  
   Corsica pays €15M annually to the water agency in Lyon, recovering only half in actual infrastructure subsidies due to technocratic procedural hurdles.
10. **Veolia / Kyrnolia Private Water Concession : €60.77M / year**  
    The private concessionaire bills €60.8M annually to distribute natural raw water captured from public mountain reservoirs, with network leaks exceeding 35% charged to consumers.
11. **The €9.5 Billion Bank Deposit Drain**  
    Banque de France data certifies that Corsican residents and businesses hold **€9.5 billion in bank deposits**, including **€5.5 billion in non-interest-bearing checking accounts**. These assets are transferred nightly to central Parisian bank treasuries to finance global capital markets rather than regional lending.

   Enforce Article L. 4425-26 of the CGCT to reclaim the **€78.2M annual shortfall** on maritime continuity and reduce resident transit fares.
4. **Territorialization of Corporate Income Tax (IS) :**  
   Mandate that retail distribution chains, banks, and utility operators file taxes where their revenues are generated, returning **€39.8M per year** to the local economy.
5. **Emergency Land Sovereignty & Resident Status :**  
   Permanently abolish the January 1, 2038 probate deadline on 290,000 family land plots and institute a resident status to shield the island from speculative acquisition.
