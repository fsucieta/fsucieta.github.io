## I. The Vulnerability of Subsea Fiber Optic Gateways

In the era of comprehensive digital governance, commerce, and essential public services, Corsica's communication lifeline depends on fragile glass strands resting on the Mediterranean seabed. The island is connected to the global Internet by only three subsea fiber optic cables linking Bastia and Ajaccio to Marseille and Nice (operated by Orange and the public initiative consortium).

This critical infrastructure presents catastrophic failure risks. In the event of an accidental anchor severance by a commercial cargo ship or subsea sabotage in the Tyrrhenian Sea, Corsica would be disconnected from the digital world within seconds: electronic bank card payments frozen, hospital data unreachable, transport bookings paralyzed, and emergency administration halted.

## II. Absence of a Sovereign Datacenter & The Flight of Island Data

The second dimension of digital vulnerability concerns **data sovereignty (Sovereign Cloud)**. The Collectivité de Corse, departmental councils, intercommunal bodies, and municipalities generate massive volumes of sensitive data daily: property cadastres, vital statistics, ARS healthcare files, and local taxation databases.

Yet, lacking a secure regional sovereign datacenter on Corsican soil, over **92% of these public records are hosted on remote servers in the Paris region or with American cloud providers subject to the US CLOUD Act (Amazon AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud)**. Corsican local authorities pay millions in subscription fees to store their own digital heritage outside their territory.

> « Over 92% of public digital records generated by Corsican public administrations are stored on physical servers located outside the island territory, depriving Corsica of control over its intangible assets. »

## III. Rural Mobile White Zones and the 5G Monoculture

While coastal luxury beach resorts receive high-speed 5G antennas to cater to summer vacationers, dozens of interior villages (Castagniccia, Niolu, Alta Rocca, Deux-Sevi) remain classified as « white zones » or endure erratic 3G/4G connectivity, severely penalizing rural remote workers and telemedicine access.

## VI. Forensic Analysis of Administrative Decrees & Telecommunication Policy

Scrutiny of digital concession contracts reveals structural dependencies:

1. **Analysis of Public Initiative Network (RIP) Concessions :** Concession agreements fail to require local server replication for critical public administrative services.
2. **Scrutiny of 5G Mobile Tower Deployment Plans :** Telecom operators concentrate capital expenditures exclusively in high-density seasonal tourist corridors.
3. **Audit of Regional Healthcare Cloud Security :** Patient hospital data from Bastia and Ajaccio is stored in Parisian private servers without encrypted local sovereign backups.

## VII. Citizen FOIA (CADA) Recourse Framework for Digital Transparency

Under Article L. 311-1 CRPA, citizens can demand:

1. **Certified Subsea Fiber Redundancy Audits (ARCEP) :** Technical traffic rerouting capacity files.
2. **Local Government Cloud Hosting Contracts (CdC) :** Service level agreements signed with US and mainland cloud providers.
3. **Rural Broadband Investment Acceleration Ledgers (ANCT) :** Public funds allocated to rural mountain fiber connections.
4. **Cybersecurity Incident Logs (ANSSI) :** State audits regarding cyber-attack vulnerabilities on island public utilities.

## VIII. Institutional Actors & Networks of Influence Mapping

The investigation into **Digital Infrastructure** reveals key institutional entities:

- **ARCEP (Regulatory Authority for Electronic Communications) :** Regulating national telecom operators under centralized market frameworks.
- **National Telecom Cartels (Orange, SFR, Bouygues, Free) :** Prioritizing seasonal commercial profitability over universal island coverage.
- **ANSSI (National Cybersecurity Agency) :** Defining national digital security doctrine without dedicated insular infrastructure funding.
- **Collectivité de Corse (Corsica Fibra) :** Deploying the public fiber network while lacking direct legislative telecom powers.
- **Rural Citizens & Local Tech Entrepreneurs :** Constrained by poor connectivity and absence of local digital infrastructure.
