Investigation 25 : Petitioner Transparency & Environmental Impact Assessments

Radiography of public inquiries : how straw men and project salami-slicing neutralize environmental assessments before the MRAe.

To bypass mandatory environmental impact assessments before the Regional Environmental Authority Mission (MRAe), certain developers utilize project 'salami-slicing' (fractionation). An investigation into unmasking true beneficial owners behind corporate planning vehicles.

I. The Principle of Environmental Assessment and MRAe Thresholds

Under European Union directives and Article L. 122-1 of the French Environmental Code, any large-scale real estate development project likely to exert significant environmental impacts (destruction of protected species, damage to wetlands, soil sealing, flood hazard aggravation) must undergo a prior environmental impact assessment submitted for public scrutiny to the Regional Environmental Authority Mission (MRAe).

This environmental assessment is a comprehensive legal document requiring four-season biodiversity inventories, a 30-day public inquiry, and rigorous mitigation measures under the ERC framework (Avoid, Reduce, Compensate / Éviter, Réduire, Compenser).

II. The Tactic of Project « Salami-Slicing » and Straw Men

To evade statutory environmental impact thresholds and avoid public consultation, certain private developers deploy the fraudulent tactic of project salami-slicing (fractionation). A 10-hectare coastal estate destined to host 60 luxury villas is deliberately not filed as a unified development permit subject to mandatory impact assessment.

Instead, the developer subdivides the estate among 4 or 5 different newly incorporated SCIs held by straw men or family members. Each shell company files a separate application for 10 or 12 housing units just beneath the mandatory MRAe submission thresholds. The cumulative ecological destruction on the wetland or watershed is completely concealed by this artificial division.

« The fractionation of real estate projects into multiple separate permits under shell SCIs is the primary mechanism used to bypass ecological impact assessments on the Corsican coastline. »

III. Unmasking Straw Men via the Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE)

This is where public access to the National Register of Beneficial Owners (RBE) maintained by the INPI becomes a powerful forensic tool. By cross-referencing company registration numbers (SIREN) of the filing SCIs, citizen investigators demonstrate that the ultimate beneficial owners holding over 25% of capital are identical physical individuals or financial holdings.

Armed with this proof, administrative courts can strike down the fractionated permits as deliberate fraudulent circumvention of environmental law (Fraude à la Loi).

V. Legal Recommendations & Citizen FOIA (CADA) Actions on Environmental Records

To demand environmental transparency, citizens can execute four statutory FOIA petitions under Article L. 311-1 CRPA:

📌 CADA Action #1 : Demand MRAe Case-by-Case Exemption Screening Sheets

Demand from the MRAe de Corse the administrative screening forms (Formulaires de Demande au Cas par Cas) submitted by developers.

📌 CADA Action #2 : Demand INPI RBE Beneficial Ownership Filings

Demand from corporate registry desks certified beneficial ownership disclosures for all real estate development companies active in the municipality.

📌 CADA Action #3 : Demand Municipal Contiguous Land Assembly Records

Request from town hall planning services records of contiguous parcel acquisitions filed by related corporate entities over 5 years.

📌 CADA Action #4 : Demand Biodiversity Compensation Execution Audits

Petition the DREAL de Corse for verified field inspection reports regarding ecological compensation measures promised by developers.

VI. Forensic Analysis of Administrative Decrees & Environmental Guidelines

Scrutiny of environmental impact assessments reveals systemic deficiencies:

  1. Analysis of Case-by-Case Screening Applications : Developers routinely submit inaccurate baseline environmental statements, omitting the verified presence of protected species (Tortue d’Hermann, Milan Royal).
  2. Scrutiny of Environmental Authority Opinions : MRAe opinions are categorized as merely consultative, allowing municipal mayors to approve projects despite scathing environmental critiques.
  3. Audit of Cumulative Impact Studies : Environmental evaluations examine each plot in isolation, deliberately ignoring adjacent developments approved within the same micro-watershed.

VII. Citizen FOIA (CADA) Recourse Framework for Environmental Integrity

Under Article L. 311-1 CRPA, citizens can demand:

  1. Certified Environmental Assessment Screening Files (MRAe) : Complete developer submissions and agency responses.
  2. Protected Species Derogation Applications (DREAL) : Authorizations requested to destroy protected animal or plant habitats.
  3. Public Inquiry Commissioner Reports (Prefecture) : Citizens’ observations and commissioner conclusions.
  4. Post-Development Ecological Compensation Tracking Registers (DDTM) : Verification of promised conservation offsets.

VIII. Institutional Actors & Networks of Influence Mapping

The investigation into Environmental Impact Transparency reveals key institutional entities:

  • MRAe Corse (Regional Environmental Authority Mission) : Delivering expert independent environmental opinions on public plans and major projects.
  • DREAL de Corse & Environmental Inspectorates : Reviewing case-by-case screening files and inspecting protected sites.
  • Speculative Property Developers & Offshore Holdings : Creating shell companies to fractionate development impacts.
  • Environmental Defense Associations (U Levante, etc.) : Utilizing INPI corporate data to expose fraudulent project divisions in court.
  • Public Inquiry Commissioners : Independent magistrates gathering citizen feedback during statutory consultation periods.
