The Plunder of the Corsican Forest & Raw Timber Exportation

Dispossession of forestry wealth : the massive export of raw Corsican Laricio pine logs to Italy without local processing or island value creation.

Home to legendary forests of Laricio pine, holm oak, and sweet chestnut (Vizzavona, Marmano, Bavella, Tartagine), Corsica is witnessing the systematic extraction of its forest wealth. Thousands of cubic meters of prime timber are felled and exported as raw unworked logs to the mainland and Italy without any industrial value creation on the island.

I. The Tragedy of the Corsican Timber Industry : An Exceptional Heritage Undervalued

Corsica’s forest covers over 500,000 hectares, representing nearly 58% of the island’s total surface area. Among its emblematic species is the Corsican Laricio Pine (Pinus nigra laricio), a majestic tree capable of exceeding 40 meters in height and providing timber of exceptional mechanical strength and natural durability, prized since antiquity for naval shipbuilding and master carpentry. Alongside this noble species stand extensive forests of holm oak, cork oak, and sweet chestnut.

However, forensic examination of the insular timber sector reveals an economic and ecological disaster. Due to the complete absence of modern industrial sawmills, kiln-drying units, and engineered wood processing lines (glulam, structural framing, certified joinery) on Corsican soil, island forest commons are subjected to a raw colonial extraction model.

II. The Drainage of Raw Logs : Trucking to Italy and the Mainland

Every week, dozens of heavy logging trucks navigate the mountain passes of the Niolu, Venacais, Castagniccia, and Alta Rocca to load freshly felled raw logs. These unprocessed trunks are transported to the commercial ports of Bastia and Ajaccio, loaded onto cargo ships, and delivered to industrial sawmills in Tuscany, Liguria, or southern France.

There, Corsican timber is sawn, dried, planed, and converted into high-value engineered beams, luxury furniture, and flooring. Worse still: Corsican construction firms, carpenters, and local authorities then re-import this identical wood as finished construction materials, paying 5 to 8 times more than the initial sale price at the edge of the island forest!

« Over 75% of commercial timber harvested from municipal and state forests in Corsica leaves the island as raw, unprocessed logs. Local added value and manufacturing jobs are systematically erased. »

III. The Responsibility of the ONF and the Lack of Local Investment

The National Forests Office (ONF), which manages state and municipal public forests under the forestry regime, prioritizes standing timber sales through public auctions. Auction lot sizing favors large multinational timber traders capable of rapidly extracting massive volumes, systematically eliminating local artisanal sawyers who lack heavy liquidity reserves.

Furthermore, state capital investment in forest accessibility roads, fire prevention corridors, and selective thinning has dropped by 45% over the past decade, increasing catastrophic wildfire vulnerability.

VI. Forensic Analysis of Administrative Decrees & ONF Directives

Scrutiny of forestry concession orders reveals structural deficiencies:

  1. Analysis of ONF Standing Timber Auction Covenants : Clauses fail to include any local processing requirements (Clause de Transformation Locale), permitting unrestrained raw export.
  2. Scrutiny of Wood Construction Public Procurement : Regional public tenders fail to mandate certified local Corsican wood species, favoring imported industrial Scandinavian pine.
  3. Audit of Forest Fire Protection (PIDAF) Subsidies : State subsidies are channeled into fire suppression rather than active economic forest stewardship and localized forestry employment.

VII. Citizen FOIA (CADA) Recourse Framework for Forest Sovereignty

Under Article L. 311-1 CRPA, citizens can demand the following four administrative files:

  1. Certified ONF Timber Sales Ledgers (ONF) : Volume, species, and sale prices for all communal forest plots logged over the past 5 years.
  2. DRAAF Industrial Timber Processing Grants (DRAAF) : Allocation records of state modernization subsidies for local island sawmills.
  3. Regional Forest Fire Prevention Plans (DDTM) : Budget execution files for mountain forest access track maintenance.
  4. Communal Deliberations on Forestry Concessions (Mairies) : Municipal council votes approving ONF logging schedules.

VIII. Institutional Actors & Networks of Influence Mapping

The investigation into Forestry Commons reveals key institutional stakeholders:

  • ONF (Office National des Forêts) : Centralized state operator managing communal and domanial forests, prioritizing raw auction yields.
  • DRAAF & Ministry of Agriculture : Overseeing agricultural and forestry subsidies without island-specific processing incentives.
  • Mainland & Italian Timber Traders : Capturing raw Laricio pine logs at depressed stumpage prices.
  • Collectivité de Corse (CdC) : Holds statutory forestry competencies through the PRFB but lacks direct operational control over ONF auctions.
  • Local Forestry Artisans & Sawyers : Under-capitalized and facing unfair competition from large external conglomerates.
