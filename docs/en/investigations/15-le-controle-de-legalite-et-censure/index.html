Investigation 15 : Legality Control & The Censorship of Local Municipal Deliberations

Radiography of prefectoral oversight : how state appeals strike down rural mayors' decrees while turning a blind eye to massive coastal real estate developments.

Theoretical guarantors of legality control over local government decisions, prefectoral services in Corsica enforce a two-tier administrative justice system. An investigation into the systematic censorship of small rural village decrees versus the leniency granted to questionable mega-projects on the coastline.

I. Legality Control : An Instrument of Selective Political Pressure

Since the decentralization laws of 1982, prior state approval over local municipalities has been replaced by a posteriori legality control (contrôle de légalité). Mayors vote resolutions and issue building permits, which become enforceable upon transmission to the prefecture. If the Prefect considers an act unlawful (under the Urban Planning Code, Coastal Law, or PADDUC), they hold a 2-month statutory window to file an appeal (déféré préfectoral) before the Administrative Court of Bastia.

However, examining 15 years of case law from the Administrative Court of Bastia exposes severe institutional asymmetry. Legality control has been transformed into an instrument of selective political coercion in the hands of the central prefectoral corps.

II. Severe Rigor on Rural Mayors vs Benevolence for Mega-Developments

On one side, the prefectures of Ajaccio and Bastia enforce absolute procedural intolerance against small inland and mountain villages. The slightest municipal decree aimed at regularizing an ancestral stone barn, opening a communal track, or granting a building permit for a young local farmer’s primary home is met with an immediate prefectoral cancellation appeal paired with emergency suspension petitions.

On the other side, when massive coastal developments backed by powerful mainland conglomerates are involved (hundreds of luxury units, seafront resort complexes), state DDTM services frequently look away or allow the 2-month appeal deadline to expire quietly, rendering unlawful permits legally unassailable via the state.

« Environmental defense associations (U Levante, Garab) are forced to compensate for state failures by suing unlawful coastal building permits themselves. »

III. The Freezing of Rural Communal Maps and Local PLUs

The second lever of administrative strangulation involves local urban planning documents (PLU and Communal Maps). To prevent interior villages from retaining their youth and building affordable housing, the DREAL and DDTM enforce extreme zoning restrictions, interpreting Coastal Law and PADDUC maximally in rural mountains while granting administrative dispensations in speculative coastal zones.

VI. Forensic Analysis of Administrative Decrees & Legality Rulings

Scrutiny of prefectoral appeal practices reveals systematic discrepancies:

  1. Analysis of Rural Building Permit Cancellations : Appeals against local family homes rely on hyper-literal readings of Article L. 111-3 of the Urban Planning Code, denying basic living space extensions.
  2. Scrutiny of Tacit Coastal Building Authorizations : State services intentionally omit transmission receipt challenges for complex coastal programs, allowing developers to claim irrevocable tacit rights.
  3. Audit of PADDUC Compliance Reviews : Prefectoral legality officers selectively enforce agricultural preservation clauses against smallholders while ignoring golf course resort encroachments.

VII. Citizen FOIA (CADA) Recourse Framework for Urban Planning Transparency

Under Article L. 311-1 CRPA, citizens can demand:

  1. Certified Prefectoral Appeal Registers (Prefecture) : Full breakdown of appeals categorized by rural vs coastal municipality.
  2. DDTM Building Permit Observation Sheets (DDTM) : Internal memos issued during the 2-month legality control window.
  3. State Environmental Authority (MRAe) Consultation Files (MRAe) : Opinions issued on local municipal zoning revisions.
  4. Administrative Court Judgment Execution Audits (TA Bastia) : Enforcement records for demolition orders on illegal coastal structures.

VIII. Institutional Actors & Networks of Influence Mapping

The investigation into Legality Control reveals institutional dynamics:

  • Prefectures of Haute-Corse and Corse-du-Sud : Directing legality control with discretionary enforcement priorities.
  • DDTM (Direction Départementale des Territoires et de la Mer) : Technical arm reviewing building permits and communal maps.
  • Bastia Administrative Court : Judicial arbiter reviewing prefectoral and civil society planning appeals.
  • Environmental Defense Associations (U Levante, etc.) : Primary civil society actors challenging illegal coastal sprawl.
  • Rural Municipal Mayors : Lacking legal counsel and subjected to intense state administrative pressure.
