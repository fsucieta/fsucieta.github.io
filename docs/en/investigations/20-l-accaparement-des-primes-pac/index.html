Investigation 20 : The CAP Subsidy Capture & Speculative Pastoralism

Radiography of agricultural subsidies : how the Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) surface declaration model enriches large speculative landowners at the expense of real working farmers.

Intended to support working farmers and maintain traditional pastoralism, European Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) subsidies in Corsica have been widely captured by speculative interests. An investigation into « subsidy hunters » and the destabilization of genuine island agriculture.

I. The TéléPAC System and the Perversion of Per-Hectare Aid

Since successive reforms of the European Common Agricultural Policy (CAP), farming subsidies shifted from production-based support (guaranteed prices per kilo of meat or liter of milk) to decoupled per-hectare surface subsidies (Basic Payment Rights - DPB). The more land area an operator declares (hectares of scrubland, maquis, or mountain pastures), the more European subsidies they collect, completely irrespective of the actual number of animals raised or food produced for the local community.

In Corsica, this surface declaration system based on the Graphical Parcel Register (RPG) sparked extraordinary speculative distortions. Large landowners and subsidy speculators declare thousands of hectares of impenetrable scrubland or rocky mountain ridges to pocket hundreds of thousands of euros in annual subsidies without producing a single piece of cheese, artisanal charcuterie, or vegetable crop.

II. Ghost Herds and the Agricultural Land Lockout

This surface speculation inflicts two devastating consequences on Corsican society. First, it generates recurring fraud in livestock declarations (« ghost cows » and phantom sheep herds moved from plot to plot during physical DRAAF inspections), triggering extensive investigations by the National Financial Prosecutor’s Office (PNF) and criminal convictions against networks of fraudulent declarants.

Second, this surface capture completely locks out genuine young farmers. Young producers wishing to establish organic goat, sheep, or horticultural farms cannot find land to lease or purchase at reasonable rates: landowners prefer to keep land locked in speculative leases with subsidy hunters who share a portion of the European windfall.

« Less than 15% of CAP subsidy beneficiaries in Corsica capture over 55% of the total European agricultural envelope. Meanwhile, genuine traditional shepherds struggle to survive on working incomes. »

III. The Collapse of Insular Food Sovereignty

The inevitable outcome of subsidizing acreage rather than actual production is the collapse of Corsican food autonomy. While the island produced virtually all its meat, grains, and produce in the early 20th century, Corsica today imports over 90% of the food consumed by its residents and summer visitors.

VI. Forensic Analysis of Administrative Decrees & Agricultural Audits

Scrutiny of CAP administrative rules reveals systemic design flaws:

  1. Analysis of Scrubland Eligibility Criteria (Prorata Maquis) : State criteria classify dense, ungrazeable brush as « pastoral land », directly incentivizing non-productive declarations.
  2. Scrutiny of Visual Field Inspection Protocols : DRAAF inspection frequencies cover less than 5% of declared surfaces annually, permitting nomadic ghost herd movements.
  3. Audit of Regional Agricultural Land Mobility Policies : SAFER de Corse preemption funds remain under-resourced to intervene against private speculative leases.

VII. Citizen FOIA (CADA) Recourse Framework for Agricultural Transparency

Under Article L. 311-1 CRPA, citizens can demand:

  1. Certified ASP European Agricultural Subsidy Registers (ASP) : Regional allocation breakdowns per farm structure type.
  2. DRAAF Veterinary Herd Identification Audits (DDCSPP) : Livestock ear-tag issuance logs versus certified veterinary records.
  3. Communal Grazing Lease Deliberations (Mairies) : Terms and conditions of public pastoral land rentals.
  4. Food Autonomy Development Funds Financial Statements (ODARC) : Public grants dedicated to fruit, grain, and vegetable short circuits.

VIII. Institutional Actors & Networks of Influence Mapping

The investigation into CAP Subsidies & Agriculture reveals key institutional entities:

  • DRAAF & ASP (Payment Agency) : Administering European CAP disbursements under standardized surface algorithms.
  • National Financial Prosecutor’s Office (PNF) : Investigating and prosecuting organized agricultural subsidy fraud networks.
  • ODARC (Corsican Agricultural Development Office) : Regional public body promoting real production, agro-ecology, and young farmer installations.
  • Chambers of Agriculture & Large Landowners : Defending decoupled surface entitlements to preserve historical subsidy windfalls.
  • Authentic Working Shepherds & Young Farmers : Demanding support tied directly to physical production and land accessibility.
