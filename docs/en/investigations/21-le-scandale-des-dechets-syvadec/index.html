Investigation 21 : The Waste Crisis & The Cost of Landfill Saturation — The Island Environmental Deadlock

Radiography of Syvadec and the waste industry : maritime cargo export to the mainland and the saturation of landfill sites in Tallone and Prunelli.

Facing the complete saturation of the island's few landfill centers (Tallone, Viggianello, Prunelli-di-Fiumorbo), Corsica is trapped in a first-order environmental and financial crisis. An investigation into the failure of source-separated recycling and the exorbitant cost of exporting household waste via maritime cargo ships.

I. The Permanent Household Waste Crisis in Corsica

For over a decade, residual household waste management has represented a recurring political, ecological, and financial crisis in Corsica. Each year, the island’s 345,000 permanent residents and 3 million summer tourists generate over 220,000 tons of municipal solid waste. Yet, Corsica is completely devoid of high-efficiency energy-from-waste recovery units or modern regional composting infrastructure.

For years, virtually all unsorted waste was directed to a handful of overflowing landfill centers (Tallone in the Plaine Orientale, Viggianello in the Sartenais, Prunelli-di-Fiumorbo), triggering blockades by local residents exasperated by noxious odors and groundwater contamination hazards.

II. The Aberrant Cost of Exporting Garbage by Maritime Cargo Ships

Faced with the progressive closure of landfill cells and the inability of the joint authority SYVADEC to achieve statutory source-sorting targets, regional authorities adopted an emergency bailout of exorbitant cost: exporting tens of thousands of tons of plastic-wrapped garbage bales by cargo ships to incinerators on the French mainland or in Sardinia.

Every day, trucks loaded with municipal waste bales board cargo vessels departing Bastia and Ajaccio. The total cost of this operation (baling, maritime shipping, national TGAP waste taxes, mainland processing fees) exceeds 240 euros per ton, driving an unbearable surge in the Household Waste Collection Tax (TEOM) paid by local island families.

« Corsica spends over 35 million euros every year simply to ship its household garbage by boat to the mainland. This money burned in maritime transport is stolen from essential investments in local composting and automated recycling centers. »

III. The Lagging Door-to-Door Recycling and Incentive Tariffs

Analysis of SYVADEC performance audits exposes the root cause of the deadlock: the sluggish deployment of door-to-door waste collection and the refusal to generalize incentive billing (redevance incitative). In major coastal resort towns, recycling remains reliant on overflowing public drop-off bins that are poorly maintained during peak summer tourism.

While Northern European and Basque territories achieve source-sorting rates exceeding 70%, Corsica stagnates at a low recycling rate, trapping taxpayers in a permanent cycle of emergency surcharges.

VI. Forensic Analysis of Administrative Decrees & Environmental Permits

Scrutiny of waste facility operating permits reveals serious governance issues:

  1. Analysis of Emergency Landfill Requisition Orders : Prefects routinely issue emergency decrees overriding municipal landfill closure votes, forcing extra tonnage into saturated cells.
  2. Scrutiny of SYVADEC Concession Contracts : Private transport contracts for maritime waste shipping are awarded under emergency single-bidder procedures.
  3. Audit of Tourism Sector Waste Contributions : Mega-resorts and private restaurants pay disproportionately low collection fees relative to the massive seasonal volume of waste they generate.

VII. Citizen FOIA (CADA) Recourse Framework for Environmental Sanitation

Under Article L. 311-1 CRPA, citizens can demand:

  1. Certified SYVADEC Maritime Export Accounting Files (SYVADEC) : Comprehensive invoices per shipping line.
  2. DREAL ICPE Environmental Inspection Reports (DREAL) : Groundwater contamination measurements at island landfills.
  3. Intercommunal Waste Collection Equipment Audits (Intercommunalities) : Number of door-to-door sorting bins distributed.
  4. Tourism Sector Waste Tax Assessment Forms (Mairies) : Specific waste surcharges levied on seasonal tourism businesses.

VIII. Institutional Actors & Networks of Influence Mapping

The investigation into Waste Management reveals key institutional entities:

  • SYVADEC (Joint Waste Authority) : Central intercommunal body managing island waste processing and maritime exports.
  • DREAL de Corse & Ministry of Ecology : Enforcing environmental facility regulations and collecting TGAP taxes.
  • Maritime Shipping Lines & Mainland Incinerator Operators : Capturing millions in annual freight and treatment contracts.
  • Collectivité de Corse (CdC) : Establishing the Regional Waste Management Plan (PRPGD) while lacking direct operational collection powers.
  • Local Resident Collectives (Tallone, Viggianello) : Mobilizing against landfill saturation and demanding universal recycling.
